# Energy Balance, Metabolism & Body Composition

## The Energy Balance Equation (CICO)
Body mass regulation is fundamentally governed by the first law of thermodynamics:
- **Energy In vs. Energy Out:** Caloric intake versus Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).
- **TDEE Components:**
  1. Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): ~60-70% of TDEE, required to maintain vital organ function at rest.
  2. Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT): ~15-20% of TDEE, subconscious movement (walking, fidgeting, posture).
  3. Thermic Effect of Exercise (EAT): ~5-10% of TDEE, intentional training.
  4. Thermic Effect of Food (TEF): ~8-12% of TDEE, metabolic cost of digesting food.

## Basal Metabolic Rate & Mifflin-St Jeor Formula
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is considered the most clinically validated method for predicting BMR in non-obese and obese adults:
- BMR (men) = 10 * weight(kg) + 6.25 * height(cm) - 5 * age(y) + 5
- BMR (women) = 10 * weight(kg) + 6.25 * height(cm) - 5 * age(y) - 161
- **Activity Multipliers:** Sedentary (1.2), Lightly Active (1.375), Moderately Active (1.55), Very Active (1.725).

## Safe Deficit Rates for Fat Loss (The Cut)
- **Target Deficit:** A moderate deficit of 300 to 500 kcal/day produces an average fat loss rate of approximately 0.35 to 0.5 kg (0.75-1.1 lbs) per week, or 0.5% to 1.0% of total body weight weekly.
- **Risks of Aggressive Deficits:** Crashing calories below BMR or imposing deficits greater than 25-30% of TDEE causes rapid reductions in thyroid output (T3), elevated systemic cortisol, severe loss of skeletal muscle mass, and sharp declines in spontaneous physical activity (NEAT).

## Lean Mass Accretion (The Bulk)
- **Controlled Hypertrophy Surplus:** Skeletal muscle accretion is limited by protein synthesis rates. A conservative caloric surplus of 200 to 350 kcal/day above maintenance provides sufficient energy and substrate for hypertrophy while minimizing adipose tissue (fat) accumulation.

## Weight Fluctuations and Moving Averages
- **Acute Scale Changes:** A sudden increase or decrease of 1 to 2 kg overnight does not represent gained or lost adipose tissue. 1 kg of pure fat contains roughly 7,700 kcal.
- **Water & Glycogen Dynamics:** High dietary sodium causes transient extracellular water retention. Increased carbohydrate intake increases intramuscular glycogen storage, pulling 3 grams of intracellular water per gram of glycogen.
- **Tracking Rule:** Body weight must always be evaluated using a 7-day smoothed moving average or weekly delta rather than reacting to single-day scale fluctuations.
